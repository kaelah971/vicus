## 1. Messaging strategy

### The central idea

Tokenized assets can be technically real and still have nowhere to belong.

Vicus gives every asset a place where people can understand it, follow it, prove relevant participation, contribute useful knowledge, and receive transparent recognition or eligible rewards.

### The communication job

Every Vicus message should help the audience understand:

1. What Vicus is.
2. Who it is for.
3. What problem it solves.
4. What the user can do next.
5. Why Vicus is different from a wallet or passive RWA dashboard.
6. What evidence makes the promise believable.
7. What is live versus planned.

### The strategic tension

Vicus resolves:

> **Serious financial infrastructure without institutional distance.**

It should feel:

- Warm enough for community.
- Serious enough for issuers and financial infrastructure.
- Exact enough for verification, rewards, and transaction states.
- Calm enough to avoid speculative or gamified noise.

### Strategic enemy

**The cold ledger:** assets are visible, but context, belonging, and human participation are absent.

Vicus does not attack finance or seriousness. It challenges the assumption that seriousness requires emotional distance and that community requires noise.

## 3. Positioning

### Internal positioning statement

> For crypto-curious users, Stellar participants, and people learning about tokenized assets, Vicus is the community and campaign layer that makes assets understandable and participatory. Unlike wallets and passive RWA dashboards, Vicus combines watch-first discovery, source-backed Asset Passports, privacy-safe role verification, proof-based missions, and transparent Stellar reward receipts.

### Short positioning version

> Vicus is where people understand and participate in tokenized asset communities.

### Differentiation

Vicus combines four mechanisms that usually live apart:

1. **Watch-first discovery** — people can receive value before connecting a wallet.
2. **Asset Passport** — every circle begins with structured context and source-backed eligibility.
3. **Proof-based participation** — roles, missions, and contributions matter more than empty engagement.
4. **Stellar-native settlement evidence** — reward and claim states are shown transparently when implemented.

### What Vicus is not

Use this contrast when the category is unclear:

- Not a brokerage.
- Not a custody service.
- Not an investment adviser.
- Not a lending protocol.
- Not a replacement for a wallet.
- Not a generic social network.
- Not a speculative asset-ranking product.

### Why believe it

The message is credible when the product can show:

- Source-linked Asset Passports.
- Explicit eligibility and risk notes.
- Watch mode before wallet connection.
- Onchain Stellar reads for role verification.
- Public role badges without exact balances.
- Proof-of-understanding missions.
- Admin review and explicit moderation states.
- Real claimable balances or payouts where implemented.[7]
- Receipts that separate eligibility, approval, claim creation, submission, and confirmation.

## 4. Audience messaging

### Primary audience: the curious participant

**Who they are:** Crypto-curious users, Stellar users, RWA learners, and holders who want context before action.

**What they need:** A clear explanation of what an asset represents, what they can do around it, and what remains private.

**Message:**

> Explore an asset community before you connect a wallet. Read what the asset represents, follow the circle, and take the next step when it makes sense.

**Primary CTA:** Explore circles

### Verified participant

**Who they are:** Someone with a Stellar address, balance, or trustline relationship that may qualify for a role.

**What they need:** A way to prove relevant participation without publishing an exact public balance.

**Message:**

> Prove the role that matters without turning your wallet into a public balance sheet.

**Primary CTA:** Verify your role

### Contributor or researcher

**Who they are:** Someone who writes explainers, submits research, answers missions, or shares useful updates.

**What they need:** Clear evidence requirements, transparent review, and visible recognition for useful work.

**Message:**

> Turn useful understanding into a contribution people can see, review, and remember.

**Primary CTA:** Start a mission

### Issuer or campaign manager

**Who they are:** An asset project, issuer, wallet, ecosystem, sponsor, or campaign operator.

**What they need:** Educated distribution, structured missions, reward controls, and participation analytics.

**Message:**

> Run campaigns that create informed attention around your asset—not just impressions.

**Primary CTA:** Create a campaign

**Who they are:** A time-constrained evaluator looking for technical execution, meaningful Stellar use, originality, impact, UX, and presentation quality.[1]

**What they need:** A product they can understand quickly and a complete workflow they can trust.

**Message:**

**Primary CTA:** Watch the full loop

## 10. Objection handling

### “Is Vicus a wallet?”

No. Vicus is the community and campaign layer around tokenized assets. A wallet may be used for verification or rewards, but Vicus’s job is context and participation.

### “Can I buy the asset here?”

Not necessarily. Each Asset Passport states which actions are supported and what eligibility or issuer restrictions apply. Vicus is not a brokerage.

### “Why should I connect my wallet?”

You do not need to connect one to browse or watch. Connect or paste an address only when you want to verify a role or complete an eligible action.

### “What happens to my balance?”

Vicus should display only the minimum role or eligibility result needed for the product. Exact balances are not public by default.

### “Are rewards guaranteed?”

No. Reward eligibility, approval, claimability, and confirmation are separate states defined by each campaign.

### “Why not use a Discord group?”

A group can create conversation, but it does not naturally provide structured asset context, role verification, proof-of-understanding, campaign rules, or transparent Stellar receipts.

### “Why not just use an RWA dashboard?”

A dashboard can show that an asset exists. Vicus helps people understand it, return to it, contribute around it, and prove meaningful participation.

### “Is this investment advice?”

No. Vicus is an educational and community product. It should not rank assets as investments or promise returns.

## 11. Claims discipline

### Use when evidenced

- Explore tokenized assets on Stellar.
- Read a source-linked Asset Passport.
- Watch a circle without connecting a wallet.
- Verify a relevant role without displaying an exact public balance.
- Create a mission and track campaign activity.
- Claim a reward through a Stellar transaction, only when the transaction path is real.
- View a receipt with a transaction hash or claimable-balance identifier, only when one exists.

### Avoid without precise evidence

- Guaranteed rewards.
- Instant settlement.
- Safe assets.
- Risk-free participation.
- Autonomous finance.
- Universal access.
- Own the future.
- The best RWA platform.
- All tokenized assets in one place.
- Passive income.
- Alpha.
- Frictionless finance.
- Trustless, when a human review or approval exists.
- Complete, when only an offchain approval has occurred.

### Capability labels

Use these labels internally and, where useful, in public documentation:

- **Shipped:** implemented and exercised.
- **Target:** specified or being built.
- **Roadmap:** intentionally deferred.
- **Hypothesis:** requires user, issuer, or market validation.

Never describe future cross-chain profiles, CCTP-funded treasuries, smart-wallet onboarding, creator marketplaces, or advanced seasonal mechanics as live unless they have been built and verified.

## 12. Voice applied to messaging

Vicus speaks like a clear, well-informed guide who respects user agency. It can explain financial infrastructure without sounding like a bank memo or a crypto promoter.

### Five message behaviours

#### 1. Explain before inviting

**Use:** “Read what this asset represents before deciding whether to verify your role.”

**Avoid:** “Connect now to unlock everything.”

#### 2. Be precise about state

**Use:** “Your reward is approved. It has not been claimed yet.”

**Avoid:** “You’re all set!” when settlement has not occurred.

#### 3. Invite curiosity, not speculation

**Use:** “Compare how these two assets are designed to be used.”

**Avoid:** “Find the next asset before it takes off.”

#### 4. Show the human reason

**Use:** “A watcher badge lets you follow a circle before you connect a wallet.”

**Avoid:** “Deploy a permissionless social identity primitive.”

#### 5. Stay calm under uncertainty

**Use:** “We could not verify that role from this address. Check the asset and network, then try again.”

**Avoid:** “Invalid user.”

### Tone by context

- **Onboarding:** warm, simple, low-pressure.
- **Asset explanation:** neutral, concrete, source-led.
- **Verification:** reassuring, privacy-aware, procedural.
- **Reward state:** exact and transparent; never celebrate before confirmation.
- **Issuer tools:** concise, operational, metric-literate.
- **Moderation:** firm, respectful, evidence-based.
- **Errors:** calm, actionable, non-accusatory.
- **Campaign promotion:** energetic but never speculative.

### Cadence

- Use short sentences for actions and state.
- Keep one idea per paragraph.
- Prefer concrete verbs: explore, watch, verify, explain, submit, approve, claim.
- Use technical terms only when they help a decision.
- Keep labels in sentence case.
- Use numbers and units precisely.
- Put the user action at the end of explanatory copy.

### Vocabulary bank

**Use:**

`circle`, `passport`, `watch`, `role`, `verify`, `source`, `mission`, `contribution`, `proof`, `eligible`, `approved`, `claimable`, `confirmed`, `issuer`, `campaign`, `learn`, `participate`, `receipt`

**Use carefully:**

`holder`, `ownership`, `reward`, `treasury`, `yield`, `exposure`, `liquidity`, `settlement`

**Avoid:**

`moon`, `alpha`, `guaranteed`, `risk-free`, `effortless wealth`, `passive income`, `frictionless`, `instant`, `trustless` when human review exists, `autonomous` when a user authorises the action, `safe asset`, `best asset`

## 13. Product-state copy

Use the same state language throughout the interface, marketing, and demo.

### Entry and discovery

- **Watch-only:** “You can follow this circle without connecting a wallet.”
- **Verification available:** “Verify a relevant Stellar role when you are ready.”
- **Restricted:** “This circle is educational or issuer-dependent. Check the Passport for supported actions.”

### Verification

- **Checking:** “Checking the role information required for this circle.”
- **Verified:** “Your role is verified.”
- **Not verified:** “We could not verify that role from this address.”
- **Privacy note:** “Your public profile shows the role, not your exact balance.”

### Missions

- **Open:** “Complete the mission to become eligible for the stated reward.”
- **Pending review:** “Your response is waiting for review.”
- **Approved:** “Your response was approved. Your reward is now eligible.”
- **Needs revision:** “Your response needs one more piece of evidence.”
- **Rejected:** “This response was not approved. Review the reason and try again if the mission allows it.”

### Rewards

- **Eligible:** “You are eligible to claim the stated reward.”
- **Claimable:** “Your reward is ready to claim.”
- **Submitted:** “Your claim was submitted. Confirmation is still pending.”
- **Confirmed:** “Your reward claim is confirmed.”
- **Failed:** “The claim did not complete. Your reward state was not marked as confirmed.”
- **Expired:** “This claim expired before it was completed.”

### Receipt language

> **A reward is not complete until the state is clear.**

A receipt should always show:

- Mission and campaign
- Reward asset and amount
- Destination and network
- Current state
- Transaction hash or claimable-balance identifier, when available
- Explorer link
- Recovery or support action

## 16. Message tests

Before publishing, test every major message against these questions:

### Clarity

Can a stranger explain what Vicus is after five seconds?

### Relevance

Does the audience recognise why this matters to them?

### Value

Is the user outcome concrete rather than a feature list?

### Differentiation

Could a wallet, dashboard, or generic community product say the same thing?

### Credibility

Can the product show evidence for the claim?

### Repeatability

Can a user repeat the message accurately to someone else?

### State accuracy

Does the message distinguish eligible, approved, claimable, submitted, confirmed, failed, and roadmap states?

### Recommended first test

Show a stranger only this:

> **Vicus — the community layer for tokenized assets.**
>
> Explore asset circles, understand what they represent, and take a verified next step.

Ask them:

1. What do you think Vicus is?
2. Who do you think it is for?
3. What would you expect to do first?
4. What would you expect to happen if you connect a wallet?
5. What would make you trust it?

Revise the message before revising the brand name.

## 17. Final messaging recommendation

Lead with **context and belonging**, not rewards.

Make the category explicit:

> **The community layer for tokenized assets.**

Use the emotional line to create memory:

> **Where assets find their people.**

Make the product proof-led:

> **Explore → understand → watch → verify → contribute → receive proof.**

Do not describe Vicus as a generic social layer, a gamified finance app, or an investment platform. Its strongest position is narrower and more useful:

> **Vicus makes tokenized assets understandable, participatory, and distributable—without asking users to become traders first.**
